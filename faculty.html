Professor Eleanor Ashworth
Chair of International Human Rights Law · Faculty of Law & Governance
e.ashworth@aldenmere.ac.uk
Lacy Building, Room 3.14
+44 1223 110 421
Biography

Eleanor Ashworth is one of the UK's foremost authorities on international human rights law. She served as a legal adviser to the UN Human Rights Committee (2014–2019) and as a member of the Council of Europe's Venice Commission. Her scholarship has influenced landmark judgments at the European Court of Human Rights, and she has provided expert evidence to parliamentary committees on Brexit's impact on rights protections.

Prior to Aldenmere, she held chairs at Edinburgh and the European University Institute, Florence. She is a Fellow of the British Academy and was appointed CBE in the 2022 New Year Honours for services to legal scholarship.

International Human Rights Law European Court of Human Rights UN Treaty Bodies Refugee & Migration Law Transitional Justice

Faculty Profile
Dr. Amara Osei-Bonsu
Associate Professor of Molecular Oncology · School of Medical Sciences
a.osei-bonsu@aldenmere.ac.uk
Cancer Research Institute, Lab 2B
Biography

Amara Osei-Bonsu leads the Translational Oncology Laboratory at Aldenmere's Cancer Research Institute. Her group focuses on mRNA therapeutic platforms, tumour immunology, and the molecular basis of drug resistance in triple-negative breast cancer. She trained at Harvard Medical School and the Broad Institute before joining Aldenmere in 2018.

In 2024, her team published a landmark study in Nature Medicine on variant-adaptive mRNA vaccine architecture, attracting £18M in Wellcome Trust and UKRI funding. She is a recipient of the Lister Institute Research Prize and the CRUK Future Leaders in Cancer Research Award.

Research Interests
mRNA Therapeutics Tumour Immunology Drug Resistance Cancer Genomics

Faculty Profile
Professor Isabelle Fontaine
Professor of Medieval European History · Faculty of Arts & Humanities
i.fontaine@aldenmere.ac.uk
Pemberton Building, Room 2.08
Biography

Isabelle Fontaine is a leading authority on medieval European society, gender, and the Church. Educated at the École Normale Supérieure and Cambridge, she has held visiting fellowships at the Institute for Advanced Study (Princeton), All Souls College Oxford, and the Bibliothèque nationale de France. Her prize-winning monograph The Enclosed Garden (Cambridge, 2019) transformed scholarly understanding of women's religious communities in 12th-century France.

Research Interests
Medieval Religious Culture Gender & the Church Manuscript Studies Memory & Commemoration
Selected Publications
Fontaine, I. (2019). The Enclosed Garden: Women, Devotion, and Authority in Twelfth-Century France. Cambridge University Press. [Winner: RHS Gladstone Prize]
Fontaine, I. (2022). 'Relic Translation and the Politics of Sacred Memory in Capetian France.' Past & Present, 255(1), 3–42.
Fontaine, I. (ed.) (2021). Women Writing History: Medieval Perspectives. Boydell & Brewer.

Professor James Meridith
Cavendish Professor of Theoretical Physics · Faculty of Natural Sciences
j.meridith@aldenmere.ac.uk
Cavendish Physics Building, Office C4
Biography

James Meridith holds the Cavendish Professorship, one of Aldenmere's most distinguished endowed chairs. His work on non-perturbative methods in quantum field theory and the phenomenology of topological phases has been cited over 12,000 times. He is a Fellow of the Royal Society, a recipient of the Dirac Medal, and a corresponding member of the Académie des Sciences. He has advised CERN, the Institute for Advanced Study, and the Perimeter Institute.

Research Interests
Quantum Field Theory Topological Phases Quantum Biology String Theory

Meridith, J. (2018). Fields, Phases, and Topology: An Introduction to Modern Quantum Field Theory. Oxford University Press.
